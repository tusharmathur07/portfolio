Pulse

Most owners find out about a cash shortfall the week it happens. Pulse is built to tell them two weeks earlier — by text, with the date, the amount, and what to do about it.

What it does

Pulse connects to a business's bank, accounting, and payroll data and monitors it continuously. When a cash gap, payroll risk, or GST/HST reserve shortfall appears in the forecast, the owner gets a plain-English SMS or email — no dashboard to open, no login required.

Underneath it are fifteen financial metrics derived from first principles: Monte Carlo cash-gap simulation, z-score anomaly detection tuned to each business's own history, payroll normalization, and Canadian GST/HST reserve estimation.

The rule the architecture is built on

The language model never touches a number. Every dollar figure is computed by deterministic Python before the model sees it; the model receives verified figures and writes sentences from them. A fail-closed output gate then checks every figure in the outgoing message against the engine's own output, and blocks anything that doesn't match.

That constraint is why the rest of the work exists — property-based testing with Hypothesis, which surfaced three real bugs the hand-written assertions missed; a seven-phase adversarial stress campaign; and a math engine tested independently of the product it feeds.

Deterministic math

Pure Python computes every figure. No AI anywhere in the calculation path.

Fail-closed gate

Every number in an alert is verified against source data before delivery, or the alert doesn't send.

Push-first

Value arrives by SMS or email. A customer who never opens an app still gets the whole product.

Canadian-first

GST/HST reserves, CRA payroll rates, and data residency in Canada from day one.

The gap: a category growing fast, standing still

India's innerwear market is large and compounding, but structurally frozen. Mass brands win on affordability. D2C brands win on comfort language that has become indistinguishable from brand to brand. International brands charge for heritage rather than construction. Every player has settled below an inherited ₹999 price ceiling that has nothing to do with what the product costs to build.

I benchmarked pricing and margin structure across 10+ premium D2C brands to establish the shape of that gap for ARC: high price and high differentiation was an empty quadrant — not for lack of demand, but because no domestic brand had priced with conviction and then justified it with the product. That finding became the basis for the positioning ARC took to market.

Turning the finding into a brand system

The positioning work became the brief for ARC's brand identity, and I coordinated that engagement with SimplePlan Media on ARC's behalf — briefing the agency from the market analysis, managing the two delivery milestones, and reviewing each round against the position it had to hold: no logo dependence, no comfort clichés, and no deference to the ₹999 ceiling.

The resulting system is deliberately restrictive. It rules out discount-led growth, trend-chasing, and imitation aesthetics — three growth levers given up so the price stays defensible.
